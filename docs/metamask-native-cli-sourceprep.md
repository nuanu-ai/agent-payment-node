# MetaMask native-transfer public selector and private vendor admission

The product CLI selects exactly five names through `--chain`: `ethereum` → 1, `optimism` → 10, `monad` → 143, `linea` → 59144, and `sei` → 1329. Numeric selectors, aliases, casing variants and additional chains are rejected during catalog parsing. The binder maps each name intrinsically; numeric `chainId` in backend requests, owned contexts, private quotes and durable journals is unchanged.

Since 0.5.43 the command is a guarded USDC transfer for any connected MetaMask profile: `apn wallet metamask native-transfer --profile <profile> --chain ethereum --recipient <address> --amount-atomic <usdc> --idempotency-key native-0001`. The profile, recipient and amount are required; there is no default profile, payer, recipient or amount. The amount is admitted by the profile's active owner policy on the direct rail (per-transfer and daily USDC caps, plus the native fee cap), an owner recipient pin on that row is enforced at prepare, reservation and the last guard before the hand-off, and the consent screen shows the recipient and the exact amount. Other chain names follow the same form. The command manifest changes its CLI option from the former numeric selector to `--chain`; this is a deliberate CLI input compatibility change. Callers must update their saved argv. The three native-transfer CLI leaves are absent from the explicit MCP selection in `src/mcp-projection.ts`; the selected 122 MCP tools and their schemas are unchanged. Shared binder input conversion does not register or expose a tool. Regenerate the full cold distribution during integration; this source repair commits no generated distribution.

The forbidden-surface scanner deliberately admits the literal vendor argument `--chain-id` at exactly `src/metamask-native-transfer-adapter.ts`. This is one new private execution-boundary admission, matching the existing exact-path direct and x402 vendor adapters. It admits no other forbidden pattern and does not weaken the 500-line ceiling or adjacent-path checks.

The SDK requires its numeric argument. Its value comes from the statically validated finite private quote, after the adapter synchronously claims the owner-issued opaque scope in the private WeakMap. Forged DTOs and unclaimed scopes fail before provider construction; a second claim refuses replay. Scope/context object identity, active lock, same UTC day, monotonic and wall-clock deadlines and quote expiry fence the handoff. The owner checks the operation's journal, the profile's account custody/capability revisions, current owner policy digest/revision/activation and recipient pin, exact chain/account conflict domain, the vendor project and Guard policy bytes recorded at prepare, one durable effect marker, native/token reservations, balances and nonce.

The MetaMask Guard policy is checked for its meaning, not compared with one owner's template (decision OD-4): its `wallet_address` must be the paying wallet, the recipient must be allowlisted for the chain (and not blocklisted), the chain must be in `evm.allowed_chains` when that list is present, and `evm.outflow_limits_usd` must carry a positive limit. Other sections are ignored. The reader accepts only the block YAML subset the vendor writes and refuses anchors, tags, flow collections and multi-line scalars rather than guessing. Three vendor reads (session project, trading mode, policy) run before consent and three in the last guard; a request read is the project, the watch and the project again.

Version 2 journals (`apn.metamask-native-operation.v2`, quote `apn.metamask-native-fee-quote.v2`) store the recipient and amount in the quote and no APN home path, so moving or renaming the home voids no journal. Version 1 journals keep loading: their stored home path is kept as written and never compared, and their quote keeps its fixed recipient and amount. Package resolution finishes before the final current-context and claimed-scope checks. The scope is invalidated and removed when the owner exits. The public native adapter still cannot use a quote DTO as financial authority.

Focused verification uses TEMP roots, mocked provider/transport seams and isolated pseudoTTY fixtures. The scanner fixture proves the exact-path admission, refusal of a nearby arbitrary file, preservation of another forbidden pattern in the admitted file, and preservation of the 501-line refusal. Actual effective-user HOME and normal provider/financial CLI routes are excluded from this source-only repair.
