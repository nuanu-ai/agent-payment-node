# Arbitrum native direct RPC batching (source proof, 28 September 2026)

The Arbitrum One native ETH direct route groups its prepare reads into five strict JSON-RPC array POSTs: chain identity and latest head; balance pinned to that head; same-number head recheck and chain identity; chain identity, pending nonce, gas estimate and latest fee head; then same-number fee-head recheck and chain identity. The fee estimate uses zero priority fee. Its `arbitrum-inclusive` quote has zero separate L1 and operator charges, and its total is `gasLimit * maxFeePerGas` because the L1 posting cost is included in Arbitrum's gas estimate.

Both approval funding phases use the grouped route. Each takes five POSTs. The post-sign phase re-estimates gas against the frozen signed envelope; its pending nonce shares that batch and is ignored because the signature has already frozen the nonce. Wrong chain, changed pinned head, malformed batch, HTTP 429, insufficient funding and increased post-sign gas stop without scalar retry or a replacement signature. The existing safe-finality and nonce-supersession rules are unchanged.

The deterministic fake HTTPS transport test checks physical request bodies, reversed batch response IDs and the five plus five plus five prepare/funding POST shape. It also checks that a post-sign gas increase stops after the fourth funding POST, before send. A normal execute would add one send POST to its ten funding POSTs: 11 from approval through send. Prepare is a separate five-POST command. The 24-attempt guard leaves headroom for immediate evidence after execute. These counts are synthetic source-test results, not live provider admission, a production operation trace, or paid acceptance. No live Arbitrum RPC, wallet key, signature or transfer was used for this proof.
