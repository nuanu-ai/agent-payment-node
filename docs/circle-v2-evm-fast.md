# Circle V2 EVM Fast runtime

`apn circle evm` moves Arbitrum USDC to Sei (1329), Linea (59144) or Monad (143) with CCTP V2 Fast (`depositForBurn`, `minFinalityThreshold` 1000) for any local profile. Every input is explicit and required; there is no default profile, beneficiary or amount:

`apn circle evm prepare --profile <source-profile> --destination-profile <gas-profile> --destination-chain <1329|59144|143> --idempotency-key <key> --beneficiary <address> --gross-atomic <amount> --max-issuer-fee-atomic <cap> --min-mint-atomic <floor>`

- The source signer is the custody wallet of `--profile`; it approves and burns `--gross-atomic` USDC on Arbitrum.
- The mint signer and destination gas payer is the custody wallet of `--destination-profile` (it may be the same profile). It submits `receiveMessage` and pays the destination gas.
- `--beneficiary` receives the minted USDC and need not be a local wallet. Amounts are positive uint256 atomic USDC; the issuer cap is below gross and the minimum mint is at most gross minus that cap.
- The route table (`src/circle-v2-evm/catalog.ts`) holds protocol data only: chain, CCTP domain, destination USDC, gas billing, the destination mint gas budget, the RPC variable with its keyless default, and the log window of that default endpoint. Adding a destination is table data. Linea defaults to `https://rpc.linea.build`: `linea-rpc.publicnode.com` refuses transmitter logs older than about 128 blocks without a personal token.

Admission is each profile's own active policy. The source policy must bind `accounts.evm` to the source wallet and admit Arbitrum USDC and Arbitrum native on the bridge rail with mechanism `circle-cctp-v2 / circle-v2-evm-fast-42161-<destination>.v2` and caps. The destination policy must admit the destination native asset with the same mechanism up to the route's mint gas budget. An optional owner `recipients` pin on the source USDC admission (decision OD-1) must list the beneficiary; it is checked at prepare, at every pre-effect confirmation (source approval, first burn, mint) and at reservation. Without a pin the policy does not restrict beneficiaries; the beneficiary is still shown on the foreground consent screen and bound into the burn, the issuer message and the mint proof.

Use `approve-source --operation <id>`, `observe --operation <id>`, `approve-mint --operation <id>` and `status --operation <id>`. Financial commands require foreground physical TTY consent. MCP exposes review/status and an explicit CLI handoff for approval and cleanup.

The fixed v1 schema (`apn.circle-v2-evm-operation.v1`: 40100 atomic USDC to one fixed recipient) is read-only: no v1 operation is prepared since 0.5.43, and the v1 executing runtime is deleted. A v1 journal answers `status`, `operation status` and `receipt get`, and `observe` once it is terminal; every effect command (approve-source, approve-mint, refresh-attestation, cleanup, adopt-external-mint) refuses it. Records are validated against their own stored binding hash and their own custody records, never against code constants, so every journal 0.5.40-0.5.42 wrote keeps loading (`tests/fixtures/legacy-corpus/`).

Read-only v1 operations keep their `circle-v2-evm-fast-42161-<destination>.v1` reference and their held usage rows, which still count against caps. Shared asset usage reserves the gross USDC, source approval 30000000000000 wei, source burn 30000000000000 wei, optional cleanup 15000000000000 wei and the destination native ceiling. The worst source reserve is 75000000000000 wei per route. Finalized usage conservatively counts approved approval/burn/destination caps; the unused cleanup reserve consumes 0. Receipt proofs retain the actual approval and burn fees separately. Monad actual fees use the full billed gas limit.

Signing and broadcast have separate durable marks. A crash after a mark or a lost RPC response retains holds; nothing is signed again. A mint or approve-zero cleanup whose broadcast answer was lost is checked on each resume (`observe`, `approve-mint`, `cleanup`): if the node knows neither its transaction nor a receipt and the signer's latest nonce has not passed the effect's nonce, the identical sealed bytes are sent again, once per command (same hash and nonce, so at most one inclusion), but only after the command read MessageTransmitter `usedNonces` as 0. If the safe nonce passed it with no transaction or receipt, another transaction took the nonce and the effect is `abandoned_unsent`: `approve-mint` then freezes a fresh mint envelope under fresh foreground consent while `usedNonces` is still 0; `cleanup` re-arms the approve-zero at the next nonce. A used issuer message means someone else minted it (see "Someone else relayed the mint"). Frozen custody, encrypted private material, common owner locks, operation enumeration and idempotency bind both signing profiles. A canonical included burn with consumed allowance may free its source nonce domain for the next Circle route only after independent canonical recheck and latest nonce advancement; other rails retain the source hold. Budget holds and destination conflicts remain until closure.

Destination proof decodes the pinned V2 `MintAndWithdraw` event with net amount and `feeCollected`. A positive issuer fee requires exactly two zero-origin USDC transfers: the net mint to the recipient, then the fee mint of exactly `feeCollected` to a nonzero address, both before the aggregate event. Who receives Circle's fee is not part of the operation, so no getter is read at the receipt block (P1). A zero fee requires the same V2 event and exactly one principal transfer.

Issuer signatures may authorize mint while source finality is pending. Success requires fresh independent source finalized and destination safe canonical observations, a bound nonce and zero residual allowance. The residual allowance is proven at the burn block from receipts, not read at `latest`: the confirmed approval at nonce n set the allowance to exactly the burn amount (its `Approval` event) and the burn at nonce n+1 moved that amount (its `DepositForBurn` event), so a later, unrelated approval of the same wallet cannot block the operation. Expired attestation uses read-only `refresh-attestation --operation <id>` for the same burn and nonce before any mint mark. There is no reburn or fallback.

`cleanup --operation <id>` provides explicit zero-allowance recovery after approval without a burn attempt, or after a canonical finalized source revert. A wholly unsubmitted operation can be cancelled only after checking zero allowance and absence of every encrypted effect material. Ambiguous effects cannot be cancelled. A reverted destination mint remains held for observation; automatic retry is unavailable.

The Circle signing domains are Arbitrum, Sei, Linea and Monad. Existing standalone Ethereum/Optimism, TRON and Solana rails have no signing-domain overlap. Sei native funding must finish before mint preparation; integration must register any native funding journal that shares the Sei owner domain.

This change supplies source, generated distribution and offline adversarial tests. It does not install the runtime, change owner policies or perform payments.

Historical v1 note — Sei also had one explicit seller gas-owner variant: `--destination-chain 1329 --destination-profile evm-live-seller`, pinned to `0x991e254B5C8e0AAf6c244eaa2706BAd059809b04`. The omitted library selector and `evm-live-buyer` preserve the historical Sei buyer route. The CLI requires the destination profile explicitly. Both variants burn buyer Arbitrum USDC and mint to the same fixed MetaMask recipient; the destination profile/custody in the immutable intent selects exactly one variant. Linea and Monad accept only their existing finite payer profiles. Seller policy must admit native Sei bridge mechanism `circle-cctp-v2 / circle-v2-evm-fast-42161-1329.v1` up to 50000000000000000 atomic native per mint; its exact active grant is frozen separately from buyer source policy.

### Adopt a public external mint

`apn circle evm adopt-external-mint --operation <operation-id> --transaction-hash <canonical-mint-hash>` is an explicit public-proof settlement command for a v2 operation on any destination (CCTP V2 lets anyone relay a mint, because the burn's `destinationCaller` is zero). It cannot sign, estimate, broadcast, open financial consent or renew a policy. A caller controlled by any local APN wallet or provider profile is refused. The owned mint path continues to require its exact owner caller. The proof reads the explicit transaction, its receipt and block headers, the functional deployment configuration at both latest heads and `usedNonces` at the latest head, with a shared 160-request, 120-second public-read budget and four concurrent requests; it reads no state at an old block and no contract code, so a pruned keyless node serves it. Create-only operation, nonce and source-message claims prevent duplicate adoption; interrupted settlement resumes idempotently. A matching, conflicting or malformed public claim blocks the owned mint before consent, estimation or custody, including after an old journal is restored.

Finite V2 funding operations also support this explicit external-mint command. The proof retains the operation’s exact recipient, gross amount, issuer fee and minimum output rather than the legacy fixed amount. Adoption requires fresh FINALIZED source approval and burn, SAFE destination receipt and canonical signed transaction, the exact issuer message and attestation bytes in the transaction (verified against the attester set when frozen, and by the transmitter on chain when it accepted them), the transmitter's `MessageReceived` with this nonce, source domain, sender and body, the current deployment configuration, consumed nonce, zero residual allowance, and exact historical USDC credit in the canonical receipt. Later recipient spending does not invalidate that credit; adoption requires neither current balance coverage nor an archive balance query. Every owned mint and cleanup material, SIGN claim and SEND claim must be absent, including when an owned mint envelope was never created. The one exception is an owned attempt that can never mint (`abandoned_unsent`: its nonce was taken, or its issuer message was used while the node knew neither its transaction nor a receipt; or a fresh envelope re-armed after it and not signed since): its permanent claims stay, but its signed bytes must be gone.

V2 adoption preserves the frozen purpose, profile and gas-owner identity, fingerprint, finite policies and expiry. It grants no new financial authority and needs no policy renewal or signing consent. Source principal and approval/burn caps remain conservatively USED; only the proven never-entered owned cleanup and mint holds become released_unsubmitted. The external caller’s receipt fee is reported separately and is not charged to the local destination profile. The terminal state is external_fulfilled, with a permanent operation/message/nonce tombstone that prevents a restored journal from entering owned mint or cleanup. Conflicting proofs and any prior owned SIGN/SEND entry fail closed. No historical UNKNOWN operation outside this exact purpose is released.


### Someone else relayed the mint

The burn's `destinationCaller` is zero, so any relayer may submit the issuer message on the destination, and public relayers usually do within seconds of a Fast burn. When `usedNonces` of the message is nonzero and no owned mint landed, `observe`, `approve-mint` and every command that observes first settle it without signing, asking for consent or sending:

1. An owned mint that never left this node (unsigned, signed without saved bytes, sealed but never past its submission fence) or whose possibly sent transaction the node does not know (neither the transaction nor a receipt) can only revert: it becomes `abandoned_unsent` with the journal reason `mint_closed_issuer_message_used_externally`, and its signed bytes are discarded. An owned transaction the node knows keeps being observed and is never adopted over.
2. Once the burn is canonically FINALIZED on Arbitrum, the command reads the burn block's header for its time and scans MessageTransmitter `MessageReceived` logs filtered by the message nonce, from the destination block stamped 60 seconds before the burn up to the latest head. It reads headers and logs only: the start block is found from headers (bracketed back from the head, then interpolated), and each `eth_getLogs` covers at most the route's `logRange` (Sei 2000 blocks, Linea 10000, Monad 100, the limits of the default endpoints). The read plan is 1 identity, 1 head, at most 32 headers and at most 64 log windows, each retried once on a transient failure, on its own client budget.
3. A found transaction is adopted in the same command through `adopt-external-mint` after the command's locks are released: the operation ends `external_fulfilled` with usage finalized. While the destination SAFE head has not reached it, the command reports it found and the next observation adopts it.

The command output carries `external_mint` with `status` (`awaiting_source_finality`, `found_awaiting_finality`, `adopted` or `not_found`), the destination chain, the transmitter, the message nonce, the transaction hash and the scanned block range. When the bounded scan does not find the transaction (a relay later than 64 windows after the burn), the operation is otherwise unchanged and the next action is `apn circle evm adopt-external-mint --operation <operation-id> --transaction-hash <transaction-hash>` with the transaction found by those details.

### Historical records (read-only)

The owner's historical v1 operations are terminal (Sei `4ee24e45…` `nonce_retired`, Linea `23f54a86…` `completed`, Monad
`df077b00…` `external_fulfilled`, and the other journals `completed`, `nonce_retired` or `cancelled_unsubmitted`). The v1
executing runtime is deleted: the v1 lifecycle, custody, nonce retirement (`cleanup-nonce`) and v1 external adoption, with
the one-off cleanup85/cleanup86 controllers before it. Their records still load: `status`, `receipt get`, `observe` of a
terminal journal (returned as stored) and the shared usage ledger read them through the v1 validators and the frozen readers
in `src/legacy/` (sealed/consumed-burn and cleanup85 retirement proofs, the cleanup85 native cancellation binding and its
ledger hold, and the first public effect failure). The v1 sidecars that only the deleted runtime read (nonce-retirement
intent, authority and claims, v1 external claims and evidence) are left on disk and no longer read.

### Native Arbitrum ETH while a Circle mint completes

A direct native Arbitrum transfer from a wallet with an unresolved Circle operation normally waits for it. Once that operation's source burn is canonically FINALIZED (approval and burn confirmed, zero residual allowance), it never signs on Arbitrum again: cleanup and nonce retirement need an unburned source, and the mint is on the destination chain. A native transfer to the custody wallet of any local profile then proceeds without waiting; any other recipient still waits until the Circle operation is terminal.
